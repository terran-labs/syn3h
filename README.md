# Mira

Mira was called Syn3h until the release that renamed it, after v26.11.2206. Older release notes keep the old name; [docs/NAMES.md](docs/NAMES.md) lists the few names that still use the old one and why.

Drive to the rim of a volcano. Unfold your buggy's force-field wings and leave the road
behind. Bring a friend, pick a distant ridge, and see what happens on the way there.

Mira is a shared sci-fi playground for driving, flying and exploring strange worlds in
your browser. Buggies, jets, hovercraft and tanks share the landscape. You can play Tag,
hold a hill, experiment with gravity, or park together and watch the light change.

The pleasure is in being there: finding a route, trying an absurd jump, making room for
someone else's curiosity. Competition is available when you want it. Explore is the
default, with no score to chase.

**Play it now at [mira.cc](https://mira.cc/).**

## Find your own expedition

Mira's tiny worlds make altitude part of the adventure. A mountain fills the windscreen
on the ground; climb above it in a jet and the landscape curves away beneath you. Take
time to look back at the place you were driving a few minutes ago. Try the same ascent
on another planet and see how its terrain, water and atmosphere change the view.

Start with a small intention. Follow a shoreline until you find somewhere worth stopping.
Take different vehicles to the same summit and compare the journey. Open the buggy's
wings at a crest, then try the flight again with less gravity. Roll inside a Zorb and let
the slope choose your route for a while.

The gear is part of the playground. A grapple gives you something to pull against;
Yoink lets you grab and fling a vehicle. With friends, leave room for experiments to go
wrong. A spectacular tumble can be a perfectly good destination.

### Choose a mode

Open the **Game** panel to choose how the room plays.

| Mode | What to do |
|---|---|
| Explore | Roam, fly and experiment together. Nobody is it; there is no hill or score. The gear remains available as toys. |
| Tag | Tag the player who is it to take the role. While you are it, tagging someone earns a point and you keep the role; a ram passes it along. |
| King of the Hill | Reach the marked zone and hold it alone to score. When more than one vehicle occupies it, scoring stops until someone gains control. |

## How to play

Open [mira.cc](https://mira.cc/) in a recent browser on a computer, tablet or
phone. There is nothing to install and no account.

1. **Pick a world.** The lobby is a small solar system. Drag to look around and tap a
   planet to land on it.
2. **Take the controls.** On a computer, click once to capture the mouse, then drive with
   the keys and look around with the mouse. On a touch screen, steer with your left thumb
   and use the buttons on the right.
3. **Open the panels.** Press Esc to free the mouse and the side panel slides in: Me, Game,
   Players & Session, Vehicles, World (including sky and weather), and Performance. Click back into
   the world and it slides away.
4. **Change vehicle.** Open Me in the side panel and choose a buggy, jet, hovercraft
   or tank. It swaps where you stand.
5. **Bring a friend.** Share the address in your address bar. Everyone in the same room
   drives in the same world.

In the galaxy, Multiplayer, Settings and About open their panels directly. Use the
other lobby buttons to switch panels, or press Escape, tap Back or tap outside to close.
The galaxy stays on screen while idle.

Press **H** in the game at any time for the controls of the vehicle you are in.

### Keys

**Every vehicle**

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| W A S D or the arrow keys | Drive and steer |
| Shift | Brake |
| Space | The vehicle's special: wings on the buggy, hover or flight on the jet, a hop inside the Zorb ball |
| X | Roll inside a Zorb ball, or leave it |
| L, Enter or left click | Use the selected gear (Plasma Tag to start) |
| 1 to 9 | Choose a gear by its place in the rack |
| H or ? | Show or hide the controls |
| J | Show or hide the invite panel with its QR code |
| P | Presentation mode, for a projector |
| Esc | Free the mouse and show the side panel. Press it again to pause |

**Buggy**

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| Space | Open or fold the force field wings |
| W and S, with wings open | Pitch down and up |
| A and D, with wings open | Roll |

**Jet**

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| E, or R | Hold for full upward thrust in hover or full forward thrust in flight |
| Q, or F | Hold for downward thrust in hover or reverse thrust in flight |
| W and S | Nose down and up |
| A and D | Bank |
| Space | Switch between hover and flight |

Release the thrust keys to return to 50% thrust in airborne flight, adjustable with
**Neutral flight throttle**. Hover keeps its altitude hold. The flight default stays off
on the ground, during materialization and settling, while braking, and inside the Zorb.
Automated pilots keep control of their own throttle.

**Hovercraft**

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| W | Thrust forward |
| A and D | Turn |
| S | Pitch when Glide assistance is off; with the default assistance, it provides no reverse thrust |

**Gear**

| Gear | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Plasma Tag | Click or press L to tag another vehicle with a streak of plasma |
| Plasma Thunder | Hold until fully charged, then release. Letting go early fizzles |
| Chaser | Aim at a vehicle and press to send a Chaser after it |
| Grapple | Click to launch, hold to reel in, V or the mouse wheel to let out, G to let go |
| Yoink | Aim at a vehicle and hold to grab, release to fling. With no target, press for a gust |

A gamepad works too: the D-pad's left and right switch gear, LB lets the grapple out and RB
lets it go.

### Camera

Press **C** to step through the five camera modes, in this order:

| Mode | What you see |
|---|---|
| Chase | Behind the vehicle, following it. The default |
| Cockpit | From the driver's seat |
| Orbit | Around the vehicle, wherever you point the mouse |
| Stationary | The camera stays where it is and watches the vehicle go |
| Roam | A free camera. W A S D moves it, E and Q raise and lower it, Shift makes it ten times faster. The vehicle's driving keys are off while you roam |

| Key or mouse | What it does |
|---|---|
| C | Next camera mode |
| Right click | Jump to the cockpit, and back to chase |
| Mouse wheel | Move the camera closer or farther; in roam, change the roam speed |
| + and - | Move the chase camera closer or farther |
| Z, held | Scope: zoom in. Turn the wheel while holding Z to change the magnification |
| Middle mouse drag, or Alt and left drag | Look around without capturing the mouse |
| Mouse, once captured | Look around |

### On a phone or tablet

Steer with your left thumb: the stick appears where you touch and its base stays there until you lift. Dragging past its edge gives full steering or throttle without moving the base. Rotating the screen releases the stick; touch again to drive. The buttons on the right
depend on the vehicle: WINGS on the buggy, THR + and THR - and MODE on the jet, and BRAKE,
TAG and ZORB on all of them. Tap a gear in the selector to switch, and use the Panels
button to open and close the side panel.

### Questions

**Do I need to install anything or sign in?** No. It runs in the browser from one address.

**Which browsers work?** Automated checks run in Chrome and WebKit, including iPad and
phone-sized layouts. Those checks do not replace testing on physical devices. The game
uses WebGPU where available and falls back to WebGL.

**How do I play with other people?** A fresh visit to the plain address defaults to the
room `lobby`, with up to sixteen players. A saved session can restore your previous room.
For a room of your own, open the game with `?room=your-room-name`. Press J to share the
current room through an invite link or QR code; the invite carries its name and world.

**My vehicle started driving by itself.** That is Daydream. After fifteen seconds without
input the vehicle takes itself for a drive. Touch any control and it is yours again.

**It is running slowly.** Open Performance in the side panel and pick a lower quality
level. There are six, from Minimal to Cinematic. With Auto on, the game lowers the level
itself when frames drop, unless you have pinned one by hand. Fresh capable devices
start at High; returning players keep their stored choice subject to startup safety caps.
Auto reduces post effects and resolution first. If a pinned level stays slow after those
reductions, a notice offers the next lower level. Choose **Keep** to dismiss it for the
session. With the cursor captured, press Escape to reach the choices. The Performance
card shows Auto, pinning and current automatic reductions.

**How do I change the world?** Open World in the side panel, including Sky & Weather. Gravity, time of day,
weather, water and a few hundred other settings are live sliders, and in a shared room
everyone feels the change.

**What are the bots doing?** They follow you around by default and do not use gear. Each
has its own personality and picks its own route.

**Can I play an older version?** Accepted releases are kept; withdrawn builds may be unavailable. Add `?v=` and the version to
the address, for example
[mira.cc/?v=26.11.1503](https://mira.cc/?v=26.11.1503). The
[releases page](https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases) lists them, each with its
playable link.

**Can I use the code?** Yes, for anything noncommercial. The code and the creative work have
separate licences; see [Licence](#licence).

## Where Mira comes from

Aubrey Falconer's earlier Mars Explorer brought flying cars, volcanoes and laser tag
together. People stayed to explore and create together, and a community grew around
those shared worlds. Mira carries that invitation into a browser: arrive somewhere
unfamiliar, learn what your machine can do, and make your own reasons to stay.

This build gives that idea a procedural foundation. A seed shapes the terrain; live
settings change the conditions. The world is something you can investigate and adjust
with other people. The vision is a place for unhurried exploration, playful engineering
and the occasional thoroughly unreasonable vehicle experiment.

Read more about [Aubrey Falconer](https://aubreyfalconer.com/), explore the
[source repository](https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h), or follow the
[release notes](https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases).

## Diagnostics

Mira can be taken apart from its address bar. Each switch below is a query parameter that
changes one thing for one visit, so a fault on a device we cannot hold in our hands can be
narrowed down by its owner, one link at a time. They work on the live game at
[mira.cc](https://mira.cc) as well as on a development server.

### Link switches

Diagnostic and rendering switches. Each one is also a row in the game, in Performance, Diagnostics,
Field switches, where it can be turned on and off for the visit ("In play" says whether that takes
effect at once or after a reload). This table is written from the switch list in
`src/core/fieldSwitches.ts`; a test holds the two equal.

### Combining switches

Switches combine freely: join them with `&`. Each one is read once when the page loads, so
change the address and reload to change them. Two worked examples:

- **Is it the shadows?** `https://mira.cc/?gfx=2&shadows=0&blank=1` holds Medium, turns
  sun shadows off and puts the blank-frame counter on screen. If the flashes stop, open
  `https://mira.cc/?gfx=2&blank=1` next to confirm they come back with shadows on.
- **Is it WebGPU?** `https://mira.cc/?webgl=1&gfx=2&freeze=1&diag=1` starts WebGL 2 at
  Medium, stops the governor from changing anything mid test and opens Diagnostics at
  start. If WebGL 2 is clean and WebGPU is not, the fault is in the WebGPU path.

### The Diagnostics report

In the game, press Esc (or open the side panel on a touch screen), open **Performance**,
unfold **Diagnostics** and press **Copy diagnostics**. The report is plain text, ready to
paste into a message. Its lines, in order:

- **Version, Freeze build ID, Source commit:** which build is running.
- **Browser, OS, Device class, Input, Screen, Logical cores, Memory estimate:** what the
  browser says about the device, its pointer, its screen and its pixel ratio.
- **Renderer, Adapter:** WebGPU or WebGL 2 and why, and the graphics adapter.
- **Graphics quality, Quality easing, Quality reason, Automatic transitions:** the level, what
  Auto is cutting, why the level is what it is, and the governor's recent level changes.
- **Startup and entry timings, Startup phases:** how long loading and landing took, step by step.
- **Shader work:** time spent building shaders and pipelines, before and after landing, with
  the slowest calls.
- **World, Play:** the planet, your vehicle and the bots around you.
- **Last 60 s, In play, Lobby or entry overlay:** frame times and the longest frames, each
  with what it was doing.
- **Room connection, Music:** the multiplayer link and the score's audio health.
- **Glass, Screen edges:** the interface glass in use and the screen's safe area insets.
- **Water refraction, Force field contact glow, Headlight beam soft edge:** effects that copy
  the frame mid draw, and whether they are on or held off as not yet proven on this device.
- **Graphics, Uncaptured device errors:** backend, adapter, scene samples, and every device
  error of the session.
- **Test flashes, Blank frames:** what the blank-frame recorder saw. Where the browser does not
  let it read frames, it says so instead of reporting a zero.
- **Live WebGPU memory (gpuTrace):** with `?gpuTrace=1`, the texture and buffer bytes alive on the GPU.
- **Sun shadows:** on, off by `?shadows=0`, held off as not yet proven on this device, or on
  by `?shadows=1` where they would be held off.
- **Flash switches:** every link switch that is on, by name.

### The rule for switches

A switch added to isolate a fault ships to production and stays there, as a field
diagnostic for the next device that misbehaves. Every switch is read once at start,
changes nothing when it is absent, and is named in the copied report. A new gate that
turns a feature off on some devices gets a switch that turns it back on, and the existing
one that turns it off, so the gate itself can be tested on the device it protects.

## Under the surface

The following sections describe the machinery. The screenshots are versioned captures;
their release URLs identify the builds they show, which can differ from current play.

<img src="https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases/download/v26.11.1512/readme-lobby.jpg" alt="The galaxy lobby: nine labelled planets on their orbits around a sun" width="420"> <img src="https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases/download/v26.11.1512/readme-alpine.jpg" alt="The buggy on a green meadow under grey granite peaks on the Alpine world" width="420">

**A world from a number.** Each planet is generated from a seed and shared settings:
terraced ground, chains of cellular mountains, volcanic islands with lava lakes,
and roads laid over what the terrain allows. No terrain model needs downloading. Your machine
grows the same world as everyone else's in the room, and the globe you tap in the lobby is
painted from that very island, so the preview is the place. Terrain, sky, water, clouds,
plants, sound and music all come from code at run time. What the game still downloads is
its scripts, the physics engine, one interface font and the Buggy's model; the jet, the
hovercraft and the tank are generated too.

**A world without an edge.** The map is a tile that repeats exactly. Drive off one side and
you arrive on the other. Cameras, bots, the minimap, the network and even the motion
vectors of the antialiasing account for wrapping to keep the crossing continuous.

**The tiny-world engine.** Rendering bends the landscape around the viewer and hides
terrain beyond the curved horizon. The simulation keeps its wrapping coordinates;
planet roundness controls the displayed curvature. This combination gives a small world
a horizon you can climb above, while retaining a compact terrain and physics model.

<img src="https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases/download/v26.11.1512/readme-night.jpg" alt="The buggy on the Ice Moon at night, its headlights lighting the ice under a violet sky" width="420"> <img src="https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases/download/v26.11.1508/lava-night.png" alt="A buggy at the shore of a glowing lava sea at night, a volcano on the horizon" width="420">

**Light that agrees with itself.** The sky is physical scattering, evaluated twice: once in
the shader that draws it and once in plain code that colours the sun, the ambient light and
the fog with the same terms. The picture is then finished in one pipeline written in
three.js shading nodes: glow, sun shafts the volcano can eclipse, ambient occlusion,
screen-space global illumination, soft contact shadows, raymarched clouds that drift with
the wind, temporal antialiasing, and a colour grade for each planet. It runs on WebGPU and
falls back to WebGL.

<img src="https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases/download/v26.11.1512/readme-swells.jpg" alt="The hovercraft afloat on a rolling blue swell" width="420"> <img src="https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases/download/v26.11.1512/readme-underwater.jpg" alt="The buggy sinking toward a sandy seabed, seen from under the surface" width="420">

**Water that carries you.** The sea is a short sum of swells with one formula, written once
for the physics and once for the shader. A floating vehicle rises on exactly the wave you
see, using a shared room clock to keep the swells aligned. The same habit runs through the
project: waves, wrapping, sky maths and the parameter registry are single shared
definitions read by the game, the room server and the tests, which keeps what you see,
what you collide with and what the other player sees consistent.

**Machines with opinions.** Rapier runs the physics at a fixed step, in its own compiled
file that loads beside the lobby. A buggy that opens force-field wings and glides, a jet
that hovers and flies, a hovercraft, a tank, and a Zorb ball any of them can roll inside.
Switch between them in place, without leaving the world. Everything is a parameter:
gravity, drag, buoyancy and some three hundred others are live sliders, change one and the
whole room feels it, and a world's settings travel in its link.

**Music nobody recorded.** A seeded composer writes the score a sixteenth note at a time
from what you are doing, and a synthesiser built from Web Audio worklets plays it: pads,
choir, plucks, bells, a hall. Engines, wind and impacts are synthesised too.

<img src="https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases/download/v26.11.1512/readme-company.jpg" alt="Bots in a hovercraft, two buggies and a jet closing in on the player across the desert" width="420">

**Company.** Bots have a mind that picks a purpose by seeded personality, a navigator that
plans a route their kind of vehicle can travel, and a pilot that works the same controls
you do. Leave the keys alone and the same three layers drive your own vehicle on a tour.

<img src="https://github.com/terran-labs/syn3h/releases/download/v26.11.1512/readme-multiplayer-qr.jpg" alt="The Multiplayer panel with the QR code the game draws for its room" width="420">

**A room that sleeps.** One Cloudflare Worker serves the game and gives each room its own
Durable Object. WebSocket hibernation lets the room stop running code while idle and retain
its connections. A phone joins by a QR code the game draws itself.

**Return to an accepted build.** Accepted releases are frozen and kept; withdrawn builds
may be unavailable:
[mira.cc/?v=26.11.1503](https://mira.cc/?v=26.11.1503) will serve that build
exactly, however many follow it.

**Built in a weekend.** One person directed a team of AI agents, each with its own lane,
tests and browser checks, releasing to production through the night and the day after.

The main tools are Three.js for rendering, Rapier for physics, TypeScript and Vite.
