# The vehicle pattern

Set by the owner on 14 November 2026: every vehicle uses the same shading and texturing
system and the same force field cockpit, can be driven from inside its cockpit and look right
from there, and is built with levels of detail, few triangles and clean, consistent geometry.
The Vehicles lane keeps this file; vehicle leads keep their vehicles to it. The budgets in
rule 4 are the Vehicles lane's proposal and change with evidence.

1. One shading system. A vehicle creates no look of its own: no MeshPhysicalMaterial numbers, no bump or flake maps, no image files. It tags each surface with a role and the shared system (src/vehicles/skins/showroom.ts) builds every quality tier, the night fill and the lava glow from that. Roles: `paint`, `accent`, `chrome`, `frame` (dark structural metal: arms, struts, sockets), `canopy`, `glow` (nozzles, ports, fans: unlit accent light, strength from `mesh.userData.glow`), `tyre`, `track` (a rubber belt with chevron lugs shaded as relief; see below), `lamp` and `trim`. `trim` is the one role where a vehicle supplies its own colour, roughness and metalness, as plain numbers, never maps; use it for matte dark surfaces and interiors. Tag with `setRole(material, role)` on a plain `MeshStandardMaterial`; untagged surfaces are guessed from names, which is a fallback, not the pattern. Static parts tagged this way merge into one draw whatever mix of roles they carry (`mergeStatic`), so several roles do not cost several draws. Faceted panels: set `flatShading` on the tagged material. Stripes and two-tone splits: give the paint geometry a per-vertex `accentMask` attribute (0 to 1) and set `userData.accentMask = true` on its material, or cut the stripe as faces tagged `accent`. Emission a vehicle adds to a merged material (a motor glow, a brake lamp driven through the material's emissive Color) is kept at every tier. Colours stay live through the source material's Color object, so `setColors` keeps working. Player-facing paint sliders (paint.scaleSize, scaleDepth, seams, flakes, gloss) then apply to your vehicle for free. Tracks: the belt geometry carries a `uv` whose u runs across the belt 0 to 1 and whose v runs along it in metres; lug pitch, depth and chevron angle belong to the role (`TRACK_LUGS`), and `material.userData.trackWidth` (metres) is optional. Set `mesh.userData.trackScroll` (metres, any value) each frame and the shader reads it per object, so treads at different speeds share one material. For one instanced draw of many treads, give the geometry a one-float `InstancedBufferAttribute` named `trackScroll` (or set `userData.instancedScroll = true` on the material) and write each instance's scroll there, wrapped with `wrapTrackScroll`.

2. One cockpit: a force field, not glass. Tag the canopy `canopy`. (Canopies still tagged `glass` also use the force field by default at shading tiers 1 to 3. The client toggle `paint.forceFieldCanopy` restores reflective glass; tier 0 uses a simple transparent tint with no procedural lattice.) The shared system draws an energy canopy in the vehicle's accent colour: one draw, both faces in a single pass, bright at the rim from outside and almost clear from inside so the driver sees out. Do not author black or tinted glass.

3. The camera sits in the cockpit and it must look right from there. That means:
   - a `ridePosition` Object3D at the driver's eye (declared on the base `Vehicle`), inside the canopy, at least 0.25 m from the nearest triangle of anything;
   - a real interior: a tub with floor, sills, a cowl or dash ahead and a seat back behind, as inward-facing geometry tagged `trim`. Do not rely on a double-sided body shell to fake an inside: it doubles shading cost and lights wrongly;
   - the canopy as one closed mesh with outward normals and nothing else merged into it (the rim is separate, `chrome`);
   - nothing hidden in the ride view, so the driver sees their own nose, wheels, wings or tracks. Once a vehicle has its tub, its closed canopy and its `ridePosition`, it sets `this.object.userData.cockpit = true` and the ride camera hides none of it. Without the flag the camera hides a vehicle whole, or only the parts tagged `userData.hideInRide`; a vehicle with a cockpit should tag nothing.
   - a clear view: nothing but canopy within about 35 degrees either side of straight ahead, and down over the nose to 15 degrees for ground vehicles (the driver has to see the ground they are about to hit) or 5 degrees for aircraft, whose cowl sits higher.

4. Levels of detail, authored, with budgets. Proposed, tell me if your vehicle cannot meet them and why:
   - LOD0, the player's own vehicle and others within the full-detail distance (30 m at the weak levels, further at capable ones): at most 12,000 triangles including wheels or tracks, at most 6 draws (merged body, canopy, instanced moving parts, glow), or up to 10 for a vehicle with many independently moving assemblies, with the count shown (the tank's six articulated treads, the jet's four thruster petals). Parts that are hidden do not count (retracted landing gear);
   - LOD1, from the full-detail distance to four times it: at most 2,500 triangles, at most 3 draws;
   - LOD2, beyond that: about 400 triangles and never more than 500, 1 draw, silhouette only. It is usually its own build, not the generator turned down.
   The distances are `detailDistances()` in mergeStatic.ts, set per quality level through `vehicle.detailNear`, with `detailLevel(distance, remote, last)` to pick a level; showroom paint steps down a little before the shape (`paintFar`).
   Generated vehicles should produce LOD1 and LOD2 from the generator at lower detail, not by decimating LOD0. `staticLods`/`reducedGeometry` in mergeStatic.ts quantise vertices and are a stopgap for imported models. Switching goes through `registerVehicleDetail(root, (distance, remote, level) => ...)`.

5. Clean, consistent geometry: metres; vehicle frame +X right, +Y up, -Z forward; origin where the physics body is; closed surfaces with consistent outward winding; no coplanar or z-fighting faces (closed solids may interpenetrate in generated vehicles, but nothing may poke into the cockpit: the canopy is see-through from both sides, so the tub must wall the cockpit off from the hull's inside); welded vertices with smooth normals except where a crease is meant; static parts mergeable (`mergeStatic`) and moving parts named and separate; one low-polygon collider that is not the visual mesh.

6. No bitmaps anywhere in the vehicle, by the owner's order tonight.

7. Tell the owner when new designs can be seen. Relayed by the hovercraft lead as the owner's rule of 15 November 2026, about 09:02 PST: "Send me a global question message whenever new vehicle designs are available, including for the hovercraft, tank, any other vehicles." So, for every vehicle: when a new round of designs is verified viewable in the shared vehicle lab (live there, not merely committed), ask the owner through Open questions, naming the vehicle and the round, with the designs as the options and the lab link in the reason.

8. No models loaded at run time, by the owner's rule of 15 November 2026: binary assets may live in the repository, but the running game must not load them. Since 16 November 2026 every vehicle is generated in code (buggy, jet, hovercraft, tank) and the game loads no model at all; the runtime asset check (tools/runtime-assets-check.mjs) fails on any model request. The original models are source material in tools/models, which the build never copies. A new vehicle is generated from the start.
